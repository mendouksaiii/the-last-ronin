# The Last Shell: Five Shards of Daybreak

An MMORPG-style samurai game set across an open world of five nations. You play Ren, a turtle swordsman and the last of four brothers, who takes on 50 missions, ten in each nation, to kill the five generals who murdered his lord and his family.

All names, characters and places are original. The story borrows only the shape of a "last survivor" tale: one brother left, carrying the weapons and the voices of the others.

## The story

The tale is told as a comic book while you play: a prologue, five acts and an epilogue, drawn in panels with captions and speech bubbles. The narrator's captions belong to a blind woman with a biwa who appears at the edge of the scenes.

### Prologue: The Night of Red Camellias

The realm of Asanagi, "the Morning Calm", was held in peace by Lord Hoshikawa Tadanobu, the Lantern Lord. Five generals served him and were called the Five Fingers of his hand.

His bodyguards were the Four Shells: four turtle brothers of the old marsh folk, found as hatchlings and raised to the blade by the lord's sword master, Genryu. Daigo, the eldest, carried the spear. Shun, the quiet one, carried the bow. Kai, the youngest, carried the war fan. Ren carried the sword.

On the night of the moon-viewing banquet, the lord gave Ren a sealed letter and sent him riding to a distant shrine. At the shrine Ren broke the seal. The page was blank.

He rode back to find the castle burning and the camellias cut down whole. His lord was dead and so were his three brothers, each at a different post. The lord's sword, Yoake ("Daybreak"), had been broken into five shards, and each general had taken one as a trophy, along with whatever else they fancied from the dead. By morning the realm was five nations and Ren was the last shell.

> *"A hand closed into a fist that night," sings the biwa woman, "and the fist held nothing but ash."*

### Act I: The Nation of Reeds, and the Ox

Ashihara is a land of fog, rice paddies and empty granaries. General Kuroda Gonzaemon, the Ox, once carried the lord's shield. Now he taxes the farmers to starvation and hangs Daigo's spear above his banquet table. Ren burns the tax ledgers, frees the rice barges, relives the night Daigo held the castle gate alone, and meets the Ox at his own table.

*The Ox's death poem:*
> I ate the whole harvest
> and still the bowl was empty.
> Reeds bow to the wind.

### Act II: The Nation of Snow, and the Bell

Shirane is a nation of mountain monasteries above the clouds. Abbot Joshin, the Bell, swore that heaven itself ordered the lord's death, because peace had made men soft. It was his monks who set fire to the watchtower Shun was shooting from. Ren climbs a thousand steps through wolves and warrior monks to the frozen bell tower, where Shun's bow is kept as an offering.

*The Bell's death poem:*
> I rang for heaven.
> Only snow answered the bell,
> falling without sound.

### Act III: The Nation of Tides, and the Tide

Shiosai is all harbors, storm decks and black sails. Lady Uzushio Namie, the Tide, was the lord's admiral, and he kept her fleets chained in port for the sake of peace. Her ships closed the water gate on Kai as he tried to carry the lord out by river. She is the only general who does not hate Ren. She wears Kai's war fan at her belt and dies laughing.

*The Tide's death poem:*
> No lord, no anchor.
> The tide I chased for so long
> goes out without me.

### Act IV: The Nation of Lanterns, and the Mask

Toka is a city of endless night, with paper lights, theatres and rooftops in the rain. Yashiro Sosuke, the Mask, was the lord's spymaster and an actor by birth. He forged the orders that sent each brother to a separate post that night, so that none could save another. Ren cuts through decoys, informers and a puppet theatre of knives to corner him on his own stage. Dying, the Mask whispers that he forged three orders, not four. He never wrote the blank letter.

*The Mask's death poem:*
> The last mask comes off.
> Beneath it, to my surprise,
> there is no face at all.

### Act V: The Nation of Ash, and the Sword Saint

Haijo is the old capital, lying burnt beneath a smoking mountain. Its ruler is the fifth general: Master Genryu, the Sword Saint, who raised the four brothers. Ren walks the road of cinders past the old dojo and the camellia garden, up to the ruined banquet hall.

Genryu does not deny what he did. *"A lantern cannot light five rooms forever. I only chose which rooms would burn."* Then he tells Ren the truth. The lord knew the knives were coming, and the blank letter was his own. It was his last order to the one guard he could still spare, and it carried no command at all, so that Ren would be free.

Master and student duel at daybreak. Ren fights with the sword, the spear, the bow and the fan.

*The Sword Saint's death poem:*
> The student's blade is clean.
> At last the old pine may fall.
> Look, the dawn he kept.

### Epilogue: The Blank Letter

Ren reforges Yoake and lays it on his lord's grave, with the spear, the bow and the fan beside it. He unfolds the blank letter one last time and walks out through the gate, masterless and finally free.

> *"Some say he wrote a single word upon that page. Some say he left it white as snow," sings the biwa woman. "The camellias, at least, bloom again."*

## Mission classes

Every mission belongs to one of eight classes, each tied to a role in the story.

| Class | Role in the story | What you do | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story | Ren arrives in a nation and learns what the general has done to it | Explore, meet the people, win a first fight | 5 |
| Hunt | Thinning the general's forces | Defeat a set number of enemies or a named target | 8 |
| Gather | Earning the people's trust | Find and bring back items scattered across the land | 5 |
| Aid | Protecting the people the lord once protected | Escort, rescue or defend villagers | 6 |
| Sabotage | Breaking what the general's power rests on | Slip in and destroy a target | 6 |
| Memory | A flashback to the Night of Red Camellias | Relive a brother's last stand, or Ren's own ride | 5 |
| Stronghold | The assault on the general's fortress | Fight through the fortress and its lieutenant | 10 |
| General | The reckoning | Boss fight against the general | 5 |

## What will be built

- [ ] **Opening.** A title screen and the prologue, shown as comic book pages, leading into the world.
- [ ] **Comic storytelling as you play.** The story appears in comic panels with captions and speech bubbles: a short comic page when a mission starts and ends, speech bubbles over characters during play, and a full page for each general's defeat with their death poem. Panels can be clicked through or skipped. The tone stays dramatic; "comic" refers to the comic book format.
- [ ] **Open world of five nations.** Each nation is a land you walk around freely, seen from above, with a village, roaming enemies and its general's fortress. Reeds, Snow, Tides and Lanterns are open from the start and can be tackled in any order. Ash stays sealed until the other four generals are dead. A world map shows progress for each nation (out of 10) and overall (out of 50).
- [ ] **Quests in the MMORPG style.** Missions are picked up from characters in each nation and tracked in a quest log showing the class, the objective and the progress. Within a nation, missions unlock one after another and the tenth is the general. Each mission opens with a short story scene.
- [ ] **Eight mission classes.** Story, Hunt, Gather, Aid, Sabotage, Memory, Stronghold and General missions play as described above, assigned to the 50 missions exactly as in the tables.
- [ ] **Turtle hero with levels.** Ren is a turtle swordsman who earns experience from fights and missions and grows stronger as he levels up.
- [ ] **Skill bar combat.** Ren fights with a basic attack and a bar of skills that recharge after use. If he falls, he returns to the nation's village and the current mission restarts; no other progress is lost.
- [ ] **The brothers' weapons.** Ren starts with the sword. Defeating the Ox, the Bell and the Tide returns the spear, the bow and the war fan, each adding its own skills. In Memory missions you play that night through the eyes of the brother concerned.
- [ ] **Five generals.** Each general is a boss fight with their own weapon and attack patterns. Defeating a general shows their death poem and returns a shard of the lord's sword. The fifth shard completes the sword and plays the epilogue.
- [ ] **Rising challenge.** Enemies grow tougher with each general you kill, whichever order you choose.
- [ ] **Five distinct settings.** Fog and paddies, snow peaks, storm harbors, lantern-lit streets and a burnt capital, drawn in an ink-brush style.
- [ ] **Controls.** Keyboard and mouse on a computer, and on-screen controls on phones and tablets.
- [ ] **Saved progress.** Progress saves automatically on the device you play on. Clearing the browser's data or switching devices starts the journey over.

## Order of delivery

You will first see the title screen, the prologue, and the Nation of Reeds with its village and a playable first mission, so you can judge the look and feel. The remaining missions, nations, generals and the ending follow.

## Assumptions and limits

- **Single-player is an assumption, not yet confirmed by you.** "MMORPG style" here means the way the game plays: an open world, quests from characters, levels and a skill bar. There are no other players online, no accounts, no chat and no online saves. A real shared online world is possible but is a much larger build that needs sign-in; ask for it before approving if that is what you want.
- The world is two-dimensional and seen from above; it is not a 3D world.
- There is no voice acting or soundtrack in this version; the story is told through comic panels.
- Comic panels are drawn in the game's own ink-brush style. Fully painted illustrations are limited to a small number of key moments, not every panel of all 50 missions.
- The hero is an original turtle character. No characters, names or designs from existing turtle franchises are used.
